Each round shows two urns with ten balls each. Left always has a red majority. Right always has a blue majority. The counts change across rounds. Each labelled circle represents one ball.

Each round independently, the computer chooses the source urn with equal probability, then draws one ball uniformly from it. After seeing its colour, enter the probability (0–100%) that it came from the left urn.

Accuracy is measured against the probability implied by the current compositions and the draw. Your score is 2 × (1 − squared error), using probabilities between 0 and 1. For example, an error of 0.10 earns 1.98 points.
